Chapter 4 Overview of Omics Data Types

4.1 Gene expression (bulk RNA-seq)

Current coverage: 20,892 transcripts across 371 donors.

On the day of sample collection, typically the shipment day (see section 3.5 above), 150 islets are hand picked and transferred to 1 ml of Trizol reagent. After vortexing, the sample is stored at -80°C until shipping or RNAseq at either Oxford or Stanford.

At destination, RNA is extracted from Trizol as per manufacturer instructions and concentration and quality is assessed by NanoDrop. RNA integrity is further checked by Bioanalyzer (RNA 6000 chips) and 20 uL of RNA samples at 20 ng/uL minimum is submitted to Novogene for library prep and sequencing, on NovaSeq 6000 PE150 illumina platform, at 20M paired end reads per sample (6 G of raw data). After quality control via FastQC (v0.11.9), the raw reads were aligned to the human genome reference (GRCh38) using STAR (v2.7.9a) with the gene annotation downloaded from the ENSEMBL database (v110). Gene expression levels were counted using featureCounts (v2.0.3).

Gene counts are processed for the web tool as follows. Genes are keyed by their Ensembl ID; Entrez ID, gene symbol, name and biotype are added as annotation and may be missing for some genes. Lowly expressed genes are removed with edgeR’s filterByExpr (a minimum read count, scaled to library size, in a minimum share of donors). No variance filter is applied in the pre-processing step. Counts are normalized with the relative log expression (RLE) method and converted to log-counts-per-million (log-CPM). The data come from several sequencing batches, and all are kept: the batch effect is removed with ComBat on the log-CPM values, protecting Diabetes diagnosis so that its real effect is not removed with the batch. Only protein-coding and lncRNA genes are kept.

The raw RNA sequencing data has been deposited in the European Genome-phenome Archive (EGA) under study number EGAS00001007241.

4.2 Gene expression (Nanostring)

Current coverage: 156 transcripts across 259 donors.

On the day of sample collection (see section 3.5, above) 50 islets are hand-picked at the ADI IsletCore, washed with PBS, then lysed in 100ul of RNeasy Lysis Buffer (RLT, Qiagen) containing 1% beta-mercaptoethanol. Samples are stored at -80oC before running on Nanostring gene expression assay at BC Children Hospital (Vancouver). The assay was updated partway through data collection, where a small number of genes were removed and replaced with others, resulting in different gene codesets. Briefly, 1ul cell lysate is used for hybridization with customized gene code sets that target 142 pancreatic islet genes and 6 reference genes. Hybridized complex is then loaded on cartridge and Nanostring nCounter to measure the target gene expression. The raw data is analyzed with nSolver4.0 software from Nanostring, producing a table of intensity values where larger values correspond to higher gene expression values. Full details are found here.

Data Processing: Nanostring data come in three batches, which differ partly in the genes measured. Technical control probes (negative and positive spike-ins) are removed, and probes that were not run for a donor are set to missing. Intensities are normalized by sample median (each value divided by the median intensity of all genes for that donor) and log2-transformed. Batch effects are removed with ComBat, gene by gene across the batches that measured each gene; no biological variable is protected. Genes measured in only one batch cannot be corrected and are flagged so they are not compared across batches. Given the much smaller number of transcripts compared to the RNA-seq data, no abundance or variance filters are applied, and no donors are dropped.

4.3 Protein expression (proteomics)

Current coverage: 234 donors in two batches — Batch 1: 8,546 proteins across 134 donors; Batch 2: 10,454 proteins across 100 donors; Combined: 8,338 proteins measured in both batches.

Human islets are shipped to the University of British Columbia, hand-picked and cultured in RPMI for 24-72hrs before the experiment to allow the islets to recover from shipping. After recovery, 300 islets are hand-picked, washed with PBS and the pellet is snap frozen and stored at -80oC. LC-MS/MS analysis is performed with n = 3 technical replicates, using a NanoElute UHPLC system (Bruker Daltonics) with Aurora Series Gen2 (CSI) analytical column coupled to a Trapped Ion Mobility – Time of Flight mass spectrometer (timsTOF Pro; Bruker Daltonics, Germany) operated in Data-Independent Acquisition - Parallel Accumulation- Serial Fragmentation (DIA-PASEF) mode. Details of lysis and analysis are found in Kolic et al. (2024) Cell Metabolism.

Proteomics data come in two batches acquired at different times: Batch 1 (three technical runs per donor) and Batch 2 (later donors). Protein intensities come from DIA-NN. Each batch is processed the same way: intensities are log2-transformed and normalized with cyclic loess (limma), repeat runs of a donor are averaged after normalization, and proteins detected in too few of the batch’s donors are removed. Missing values are then filled with limpa’s exponential tilting method (Li & Smyth 2023), using each batch’s own detection curve; measured values are not changed. The two batches are also combined, keeping the proteins that pass in each batch, with no variance filter. Because the batches differ systematically, the combined data can be used in two ways: Batch corrected, where the batch effect is removed with ComBat while protecting Diabetes diagnosis (Type 2 vs No Diabetes), or Batch as covariate, where the uncorrected data are used and batch is added to the model automatically. Multi-omics integration (chapter 5) uses the ComBat-corrected data.

Raw proteomics data has been deposited to ProteomExchange via MASSive under dataset number PXD045422.

[The sample preparation and LC-MS/MS description above and dataset PXD045422 are from v1 (Batch 1); the v2 (Batch 2) protocol and data deposition need confirmation from the Johnson lab.]

4.4 Gene expression (pseudobulk RNA-seq)

Current coverage: 58 HumanIslets donors with pseudobulk profiles.

The single-cell counts from patch-seq data (section 4.5, below) were summarized at the donor by separating counts by cell type (alpha and beta). Only cells from donors with at least five cells were retained. Pseudobulk profiles were created by summing counts from all cells derived from the same donor. Ensembl gene IDs were mapped to Entrez IDs and counts from any duplicated Entrez IDs were summed. Each matrix was filtered to remove transcripts with greater than 80% zeros. Counts were converted to logCPM values using the TMM method from the edgeR R package (version 3.38.4). Due to the large variations in sequencing depth across batches, individual transcripts that had zero or one count were replaced with NA values.

4.5 Single-cell gene expression (patch-seq)

Current coverage: 61 HumanIslets donors with patch-seq cells.

The collection and analysis of pancreas patch-seq data is described here and here. Reads from FASTQ files were trimmed using TrimGalore version 0.6.5 with auto-detection of adapter sequence, then aligned and counted using STAR version 2.7.9a to the GRCh38 human genome (Ensembl release 104). Cell count matrices were loaded into R and a Seurat object was generated using the Seurat R package (version 4.3.0). Only cells with patch-clamp data were included; data for additional cells are available from the listed accession numbers below. Cell types were identified by projection, using the MapQuery command from the R package Seurat, onto a large set of human islet single cell RNAseq data generated from publicly available data (see below). Cells were filtered to remove any that were not alpha or beta cells, had fewer than 500 total counts across all transcripts, had fewer than 300 unique transcripts with non-zero count values, or had greater than 20% mitochondrial DNA. Ensembl gene IDs were mapped to Entrez IDs and counts from any duplicated Entrez IDs were summed. Counts were separated by cell type (alpha and beta) and glucose concentration (1, 5, and 10mM), resulting in six different matrices. Each matrix was filtered to remove transcripts with greater than 80% zeros. Counts were converted to logCPM values using the trimmed mean of M-values (TMM) method from the edgeR R package (version 3.38.4). TMM has been shown to perform better for single-cell RNAseq data compared to the RLE method because it is robust to the high drop-out rate. Individual transcripts that had zero counts were replaced with NA.

Raw sequencing reads are available in the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under accession numbers GSE124742 and GSE164875 and GSE270484 at PancDB (https://hpap.pmacs.upenn.edu/).

4.6 Single-cell RNA-seq

Reads from FASTQ files were trimmed using TrimGalore version 0.6.5 with auto-detection of adapter sequence, then aligned to the GRCh38 human genome, Ensembl release 104, and counted using STAR version 2.7.9a except for files from GSE84133, which were aligned and counted using the inDrops protocol; files from GSE81076 and GSE85241, which were aligned and counted using the R package scruff (version 1.10.1) with Rsubread (version 1.22.2); and files from GSE269204, which were aligned and counted with Cell Ranger (10X Genomics Cell Ranger v6.1.2). Cell count matrices were loaded into R and objects generated using the R package Seurat (version 4.3.0), restricted to transcripts detected in more than 10 cells and cells with between 200 and 12,500 unique transcripts with non-zero count values; cells with percent mitochondrial transcripts in the top 25% for each dataset were also dropped. Transcript names were maintained as Ensembl identifiers to preserve the greatest number of unique transcripts. After combining all donors, transcripts expressed in fewer than 0.1% of cells were filtered. Donor HPAP-027 was dropped from the data set because there were very few cells (32 cells). The reciprocal PCA integration with references (HPAP-037, HPAP-040; HPAP-045, HPAP-050, HPAP-052, and HPAP-072) workflow from the R package Seurat (version 4.3.0) was followed, with k.anchor = 5, then FindNeighbors and FindCluster (resolution = 1.2). Cell types were first manually annotated based on the unambiguous, high level expression of marker genes in distinct clusters in UMAP space, except cytotoxic T cells, which did not form a distinct cluster but had restricted expression of TRAC. The remaining ambiguous cells were annotated using the MapQuery function by projecting unknown cells onto the manually annotated cells. Although these data include cells from donors with type 1 diabetes and type 2 diabetes, only cells from donors with no diabetes (ND) are visualised in the web tool for cell-type localization of called features, and are not downloadable.

Data are from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under accession numbers GSE81076, GSE81547, GSE81608, GSE83139, GSE84133, GSE85241, GSE86469, GSE154126; from the European Molecular Biology Laboratory European Biology Institute (EMBL-EBI) under accession number E-MTAB-5061; from the Human Pancreas Analysis Program at PancDB up to donor HPAP-109, and from unpublished data (GSE269204).

4.7 Metabolomics

Current coverage: 215 annotated metabolite features across 38 donors (38 at low glucose, 37 at high glucose and in ratio form), measured at low (2.8 mM) and high (16.7 mM) glucose, with ratio also reported.

Metabolomics samples are collected on islets handled at the ADI IsletCore under the same two glucose conditions used elsewhere in the platform — low glucose (LG, 2.8 mM) and high glucose (HG, 16.7 mM) — so that metabolite intensities can be compared at fasting-like versus stimulation-like conditions and an HG/LG ratio can be reported alongside each absolute value. Samples are analysed by liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry. Raw mzML spectra are acquired across two batches (B1 and B2) in both positive- and negative-ion modes. Peak picking is performed with Asari (xialab’s open-source MS preprocessor), producing per-mode preferred feature tables and a feature-level annotation file. Feature tables are then processed in MetaboAnalyst (xialab’s metabolomics platform; see https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/).

Post-processing of the MetaboAnalyst output is implemented in a custom R pipeline:

  • Blank filtering: features that are not appreciably elevated over process blanks are dropped.
  • Detection (QC) filtering: features whose intensity is unstable across pooled QC injections are dropped.
  • Normalization: median normalisation (each sample divided by its median feature intensity) followed by log transform.
  • Batch correction: the production pipeline uses ComBat (parametric empirical Bayes, sva R package), with donor diabetes status included as a protected covariate so the inter-batch correction preserves biological T2D-versus-no-diabetes signal. Three alternatives were benchmarked during method selection — WaveICA 2.0 (wavelet + ICA, no batch labels required), QC-RLSC (QC-based LOESS signal correction), and median-only — and ComBat-with-diagnosis was selected on the basis of PCA separation and downstream replicability.
  • MS2 annotation: once corrected, the feature tables are merged with MS2-level annotations (HMDB IDs, main class, sub class) to produce the final 215 annotated metabolites surfaced in the platform.

Positive- and negative-mode results are combined into a single donor × metabolite matrix at low glucose, high glucose, and as the HG/LG log-ratio.

4.8 Environmental Contaminants

Current coverage: 58 contaminant measurements across 31 donors.

Environmental contaminants were quantified in pancreas and adipose tissue samples by the lab of Dr. Jenny Bruin (Carleton University). For each donor, multiple ~5 mm cubic biopsies of pancreas and peripancreatic adipose tissue were collected and flash frozen. The frozen tissue biopsies were sent to SGS AXYS (Sidney, BC, Canada) to measure a panel of 17 dioxin/furan, 16 PCB, and 28 OCP analytes using GC-MS/MS; SGS AXYS is ISO 17025 accredited by the Canadian Association for Laboratory Accreditation. Sample concentrations were determined based on labelled surrogate standard quantification and sample weight, and were adjusted for percent recovery. Sample limits of detection (LOD) were established based on a 3:1 signal-to-background-noise ratio.

Analytes that had NDR flags (defined by SGS AXYS as a peak that was detected but did not meet quantification criteria) in more than 40% of donors were excluded from the dataset. This included 2 dioxin/furans in adipose, 1 dioxin/furan in pancreas, and 2 OCPs in both adipose and pancreas, leaving a total of 57 and 58 analytes in adipose and pancreas, respectively. Analytes that passed the NDR inclusion criteria were blank-adjusted using batch-specific lab blank values (labelled “Blank adjusted” on the web tool). Data are also provided as “Blank and LOD adjusted”: for these data, if a donor had a non-detect (ND) flag for an analyte that met the NDR criteria, it was assigned a value equivalent to half the batch-specific LOD for that analyte.

In addition to individual analyte concentrations, arithmetic sums are provided for dioxins/furans and PCBs. For PCBs, the sums of dioxin-like (DL) PCBs, non-dioxin-like (NDL) PCBs, and all PCBs are provided.

Because contaminant data are typically zero-inflated and non-normal, in-tool analyses use Kendall rank correlation (partial Kendall when covariates are supplied) rather than the moderated-t approach used for transcriptomics/proteomics; see chapter 5 (Kendall rank correlation) for details. The detailed measurement and quality-control protocol is described in Hoyeck et al. (2026) Nature Communications, doi:10.1038/s41467-026-71847-5.

The first cohort for contaminant analysis includes 14 females and 17 males, with ages ranging from 25–70, BMI ranging from 12.8–40.3, and HbA1c ranging from 3.6–8.6%. Of the 31 donors, 4 were clinically diagnosed with T2D and 1 had an HbA1c in the pre-T2D range. This cohort is described in detail in Hoyeck et al. (2026) Nature Communications, doi:10.1038/s41467-026-71847-5. Additional environmental contaminant data on a larger cohort of donors are forthcoming.

4.9 DNA Methylation

Current coverage: 729,123 CpG sites across 50 donors (Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC v2.0 array).

Genome-wide DNA methylation is profiled on the Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC v2.0 (EPICv2) BeadChip, contributed by our collaborators in Singapore. Raw β-values (the methylated fraction at each probe, 0–1) are reported for 784,789 probes per donor and processed into the platform’s analysis tables through a custom R pipeline:

  • Probe filtering: only cg (CpG) probes are kept (control, SNP and non-CpG probes are dropped) and sex-chromosome probes are removed; the sesame recommended EPICv2 mask (all-ancestry union at 1% minor-allele frequency) is then applied to drop poor-mapping, cross-reactive and polymorphic probes, leaving 729,123 CpG sites.
  • Replicate collapse: EPICv2 carries several replicate probes per CpG; these are averaged to a single value per CpG.
  • β and M values: both scales are stored — β (0–1, used for interpretation and display) and M = log2(β / (1 − β)), the variance-stabilised scale used for statistical testing.
  • Annotation: each CpG is annotated on hg38 using the Bioconductor EPICv2 annotation: genomic coordinate, UCSC RefGene gene and gene region, and CpG-island relation (Island / Shore / Shelf / OpenSea). As is typical for EPIC arrays, roughly one third of CpGs fall within a RefGene gene and the remainder lie in intergenic / open-sea regions.

4.10 Metabolic Flux

Current coverage: 2,771 reactions at high glucose (2,775 at low glucose; 2,765 in ratio form) across 371 donors.

Donor-specific genome-scale metabolic models (GEMs) are built from the Human-GEM v1.19.0 reconstruction and loaded into COBRApy from per-donor MATLAB-format model files. ATP hydrolysis (Human-GEM reaction MAR03964) is set as the cellular objective. The platform simulates two glucose conditions and two media compositions:

  • Low glucose (LG): approximately 2.8 mM (the glucose exchange reaction is bounded at −2.0 flux units, matching the GSIS / perifusion / metabolomics LG condition).
  • High glucose (HG): approximately 16.7 mM (glucose exchange bounded at −10.0 flux units, matching the platform’s HG stimulation condition).
  • Both conditions are simulated under glucose-only media and under glucose plus amino-acid supplementation (with fixed uptake rates for glutamine, alanine, serine, etc.); the amino-acid scenario opens alternative carbon entry into the TCA cycle.

For each donor × condition, Flux Variability Analysis (FVA) is run on the entire reaction network at a 90% optimality fraction; the platform stores the FVA minimum, maximum, centre, and span per reaction. OptGP flux sampling (5,000 samples, thinning = 100) is run on top of FVA to capture the achievable flux distribution; from the samples the pipeline records the mean, median, standard deviation, and 25/75% quartiles per reaction. Per-reaction flux values are signed-log2 transformed and z-scored within each donor before being loaded into the analysis tables so the downstream limma model sees comparable distributions across reactions. The HG/LG ratio table stores the per-reaction log-ratio between high glucose and low glucose conditions, capturing each donor’s glucose responsiveness at the flux level.
